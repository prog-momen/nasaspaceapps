<p data-block-key="d6p4o">Since the 1960s, NASA has left hardware across the solar system–on the Moon, on Mars, and in deep space. From rovers and instruments that completed their science missions to probes still traveling away from Earth this hardware varies widely in both its purpose and current level of functionality. Your challenge is to tell the story of some or all of this equipment that introduces school-age space enthusiasts to the hardware and the science it made possible.</p><h2 data-block-key="82b9v">Background</h2><p data-block-key="81kfa">For over half a century, humanity has sent incredible machines to the Moon, Mars, and other celestial bodies. When their missions end, probes, rovers, landers, descent stages, and scientific instruments are left behind, standing—or flying—silently as monuments to human exploration. While space agencies know the histories of these machines, school-age space enthusiasts often hear only about active, ongoing missions. There is a real opportunity here to inspire the next generation using the rich, untold histories of these off-world artifacts and the groundbreaking science that paved the way for today's achievements and tomorrow’s exploration.</p><p data-block-key="4q9te">To respond to this challenge, participants will want to know what types of equipment currently reside on the Moon, on Mars, and elsewhere—old rovers, descent stages, scientific payloads—and the specific missions they facilitated. The priority is to translate complex historical and scientific data into an engaging narrative.</p><p data-block-key="d97g">People looking to learn about past space missions often work through dense technical websites or historical textbooks that lack the spark of imagination and storytelling needed to capture a younger or more casual audience's attention. Potential projects could include an interactive map of the Moon with clickable "ghost rovers," a digital graphic novel told from the perspective of a dormant Mars lander, or an audio podcast series chronicling the final days of a deep space instrument. We encourage you to think outside the box and develop your own creative alternatives!</p><h2 data-block-key="6cvr4">Objectives</h2><p data-block-key="bf2mb">The goal of this challenge is to create an engaging, story-driven educational tool or narrative that teaches school-age and casual space enthusiasts about the hardware left on the Moon, Mars, and elsewhere. Your project might bring these silent or active artifacts (back) to life, explaining what they are, why they were originally sent into space, and the scientific discoveries they made and continue to make.</p><p data-block-key="ca5qa">Your final project can take many forms, and we welcome a variety of formats and technologies. For example, you might develop an interactive web-based experience using HTML, CSS, or JavaScript; prototype an educational video game using a game engine of your choice; write and illustrate a digital children's book; produce a short animated video; design an interactive 3D timeline; or take another approach entirely!</p><h2 data-block-key="27bf6">Potential Considerations</h2><p data-block-key="b3eaf">Regardless of the medium or technology you choose, a strong narrative focus will serve your project well. Consider weaving real historical facts and mission data into your story, and showing your audience how these missions operated rather than listing facts about them. The aim is to draw a clear, exciting connection between the engineering of the hardware and the scientific knowledge humanity gained from it. By the end of your story, your audience might walk away feeling a personal connection to these forgotten robotic explorers and inspired by the science they conducted. Accuracy is important throughout!</p><p data-block-key="3r0n0"><i>You may (but are not required to) consider the following:</i></p><ul><li data-block-key="76mdb">What age group is your story designed for?</li><li data-block-key="7tadd">How accessible is the format you chose to use?</li><li data-block-key="666f6">Do you want to focus on specific missions and pieces of equipment, or tell a broader story about NASA’s exploration?</li><li data-block-key="4r4r9">What perspective(s) do you want to highlight?</li></ul><p data-block-key="2gvlb"><i>For data and resources related to this challenge, refer to the Resources tab at the top of the page. More resources may be added before the hackathon begins.</i></p>
